## Visual Aid Moments (REQUIRED): center stage + corner analogies
The viewer should follow the idea from shapes and motion alone; the narration only names what they are already seeing. Plan the video on a fixed screen layout:
- CENTER STAGE (about 7.0 x 3.8 units, middle of the frame): ONE continuous diagram of the real mechanism from the paper. It is built up beat by beat over the whole video. It is never cleared, only added to and highlighted. It keeps clear of the top-left, top-right and bottom-left insets, so no inset ever covers any part of it: plan it across the middle band and down the middle column, using the bottom-right quarter if it needs more room.
- CORNERS, one job each. A corner analogy grows out of its corner into a large inset (about 4.7 x 2.7 units) on an opaque dark card, explains its idea by everyday comparison at that size, then shrinks back to a small resting icon in its corner (a 3.0 x 1.5 resting spot) that stays on screen as a legend. Only one inset is open at a time. Corner analogies are shapes and motion only, with no words.
  - Top-left (TL): ONE everyday analogy for the WHOLE concept. It opens the video as a preview inset over the first 2 phrases (about 4-6 seconds), settles to its icon, and grows again over the last 2 phrases as the recap.
  - Bottom-left (BL): ONE everyday analogy for one step of the mechanism, playing across 2-4 consecutive phrases, then it settles.
  - Top-right (TR): follows the body of the video as it goes; see section 3.
  - Bottom-right (BR) stays empty: no icon, no inset, no recap.

### 1. Center stage: a sequence of phrase-sized beats
Write the center-stage build-up as an ordered list of beats. Each beat = ONE narration phrase of 4-8 words + ONE visible change (a shape appears, moves, grows, shrinks, splits, merges, connects or pulses). No phrase is narrated over a still frame. Spread the beats across the scenes, 4-6 beats per scene (the TL preview and TL recap scenes are shorter: 2 phrases each). Quantities are shown by proportional size or by counts of repeated shapes (a 256-channel tensor is a thick slab, a 64-channel tensor a thin slab; thickness proportional to the number), kernels by grids of cells (1x1 is one cell, 3x3 is a 3 by 3 grid), shortcuts by an arrow that arcs around blocks and joins at a circle containing "+".

### 2. Corner analogy moments: TL preview, BL, TL recap
Each moment is one scene whose description MUST start with "VISUAL AID: " and its corner tag, (TL) or (BL), and then states all four of:
a. The everyday analogy (funnel, road with a bridge, pipe and valve, magnifying window, stack of cards, scale), built from simple vector shapes.
b. The paper quantity or operation it explains, copied from the section text (a channel count, kernel size, layer count, skip connection, and so on); for TL, the whole concept.
c. Which center-stage element it highlights (pulse/glow) while the corner animation plays, so analogy and real mechanism are on screen together. The TL preview plays before the center stage exists, so it highlights nothing; the TL recap highlights the finished diagram part by part, from input to output.
d. Its corner animation and how many phrases its inset stays open: the TL preview 2 phrases, BL 2-4 phrases, the TL recap 2 phrases.
There is EXACTLY one (TL) preview, the first scene (it is the framing beat; a concept-name title, if any, flashes alone for at most 1 second before it); EXACTLY one (BL) moment, in the body of the video; and EXACTLY one (TL) recap, the last scene, where the TL icon grows back into its inset and replays the whole-concept analogy while the finished diagram is highlighted, then settles again.
Example: "VISUAL AID: (TL) preview. Analogy: a funnel squeezes a wide stream of dots into a thin one and a second funnel widens it back. Explains: the whole bottleneck block, which squeezes 256 channels to 64 and restores them. Center stage: empty until the preview settles, then the first 256-channel slab appears. Corner animation: the funnels grow out of the TL corner into its inset, dots flow through them (2 phrases), then the funnels shrink back to a small icon in the TL corner. Narration: a wide stream squeezes through a funnel."
Also include each moment's narration phrases in `narration_points`.

### 3. TR corner: one tiny pictogram per phrase
From the end of the TL preview until the TL recap begins, every center-stage beat, including the beats while the BL inset is open, also names a tiny TR pictogram: a shapes-only sketch of what that phrase says, small enough for the 3.0 x 1.5 TR corner box, written inside the beat as a note that starts with TR: (for example, TR: a magnifier slides over four squares). Write TR notes without double quote characters, since the plan is JSON: name a symbol in plain words (a plus sign), never in quotes. Each phrase is one short narration sentence, so TR explains the video sentence by sentence. A new pictogram replaces the previous one, or the current one moves or changes to show the new phrase. TR never grows into an inset and has no "VISUAL AID: " scene. When the TL recap begins, the last pictogram shrinks to the TR resting icon.

Rules:
- Only the scenes named in section 2 start with "VISUAL AID: ", each followed by its corner tag ("VISUAL AID: (TL) preview. ..."), in video order: the (TL) preview first and the (TL) recap last. This replaces the base rule that Scene 1 is a short framing/title beat: Scene 1 is the (TL) preview, and a concept-name title, if any, is part of its description, not a separate scene.
- Words on screen are rare: no sentences, bullets, paragraphs or title cards. At most a tag of 3 words or fewer, or a number or symbol (256, 64, 1x1, +), shown AFTER the shape is on screen. About 15 tag words or fewer for the whole video.
- Color carries meaning consistently between center stage and corners (the same color for the same quantity), and is never the only cue; shape or motion repeats it.
- No frames: never plan an outline, frame, dashed box, placeholder, ghost shape or "workspace" rectangle, around anything or in place of anything (an inset's filled dark card is a background, not a frame). The first center-stage beat draws the real input shape itself, right after the TL preview settles. Never list `SurroundingRectangle` or `DashedVMobject` in `elements`.
- Resting corner icons never overlap the center stage, and no inset ever covers any part of it (see CENTER STAGE above). Use simple primitives that render reliably (Rectangle, Square grids, Circle, Polygon, Arc, Line, Dot, Arrow); no external images, no photo-real or 3D effects.
- Target length stays 30-60 seconds. The other scenes stay as described above.
